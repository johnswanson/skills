# The brief's outline

`BRIEF.md` is the only document every writer reads, so it carries the whole song. Write it in this order. The
wording under "What a good lyric here does" may be copied as it stands and adjusted to the song.

## 1. The singer

For a recurring character, a biography of about 200 words: age, where they are from and what their parents did,
what work they have done, how they write and who the "you" in their songs is, what they believe (stated as things
they would never say as a slogan), and their manner. Say who or what their antagonist always is. The biography is
what makes ten writers sound like one artist, and it supplies the details the best lines are made of: in the first
run, a mother who was a librarian became the song's central motif, a due-date stamp.

For a one-off song, two or three sentences on who is singing and to whom.

## 2. The song

The situation in one paragraph: who wants what, what stops them, what they do about it.

## 3. The hook

The fixed line or couplet, quoted, with one sentence on why it is both the joke and the point. Say that its wording
should survive close to word for word, and that its place in the song (chorus, tag, or a turn that lands once and
returns) is the writer's choice.

## 4. The sincere turn

The one moment where the song stops joking, and what is true in it.

## 5. Facts

A short list of true things the writers may lean on, and the rule: keep every factual claim modest and true; invent
no statistics, laws, dates or named officials; name no real people, companies or politicians.

## 6. What a good lyric here does

- **Every line makes literal sense.** A listener must be able to picture exactly what each line says. No mixed
  metaphors, no lines that only sound poetic, no rhyme-filler, no abstraction standing where an object should be. A
  metaphor, once chosen, stays consistent, and every line works on both levels.
- **Concrete over clever.** Name the objects of this song's world (give five or six as examples).
- **It tells a story in order**, and a first-time listener follows it in one hearing.
- **Singable.** Natural speech stress, lines of even length within a section, one verse shape held throughout, a
  chorus short enough to sing back after one listen, and a rhyme partner for the hook's last word.
- **Funny and fair.** Every person and place in it drawn with affection.
- **Length**: the target in sung words (340 to 400 for four minutes), with a bridge, and every chorus written out in
  full.

## 7. Format

The lyric format rules, copied from the `lyrics` skill.

## 8. The style

The style rules, copied from the `suno-style` skill, plus: the singer's voice; whether the sound is open (say what
neighbourhood the artist lives in, then leave genre, tempo and arrangement to the writer) or given; and that every tag
names something to be heard.
